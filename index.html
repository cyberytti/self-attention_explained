Self attention

Introduction

The problem self attention solves

We will start from the basics and first understand the problem for which self attention is created. Now, we all know the fact that computers can’t understand raw text; not only text, they also can’t understand images, videos, audio, etc. We know this, and we also know that computers can only understand numbers. This is where everything starts. Many of us know that the main application of self attention was in transformers, specifically the encoder-decoder transformer for seq2seq tasks, mainly for language. Here the first question arises:

How to represent text into numbers?

In the early days, scientists used techniques like one-hot encoding, bag of words, etc., but the problem with these algorithms was that they were not capable enough to represent the meaning of a particular word through numbers.

Then later they found techniques like text embeddings, where a neural network is trained on a huge dataset to understand the meaning of particular words and their semantic meaning. Now it can easily say that there is a difference between apple and orange, but somewhere they are also related because they both are fruits. These embedding models produce something called “vectors”. Instead of representing a single word with a single int, they represent a single word with a word vector, which can carry a lot more info about that word. After getting the embeddings for apple and orange, we can simply use an algorithm like cosine similarity to find how similar these two words are. Now if I get the embedding of a car and compare the cosine similarity of these three words and plot them in a graph, that graph would look something like this

Here you can see that apple and orange have some distance between them, but they are still very close, which means they have a very strong connection. But the car is far away from them, which means the car is not related to them.

After understanding what text/word embeddings do, you can think that text embedding techniques solve our first question, but there is a huge catch with it, and now things will get deeper.

See, it’s true that word embeddings can distinguish between apple and car, but all of this is static. It is not necessary that a word X will always mean the same thing; the meaning of the same word might change.

Let’s take an example:

I will write two sentences:

  1. An apple a day keeps the doctor away.
  1. The camera quality of apple is really good.

These two sentences contain the same word “apple,” but in the different sentences it holds a completely different meaning due to the rest of the context of the sentence. Here word embeddings fail. Throughout the training, a word embedding model associates a single meaning to a single word, which can’t be changed depending on the context. For a word embedding model, both “apple”s hold the same meaning. This is the problem self attention solves.

How does self attention solve this problem?

Self attention was created for this purpose: to compute the actual meaning of a word depending on its context. The term “self attention” means how much attention one word should give to other words, which word depends on which, and how strong those dependencies are. Self attention produces a thing called “contextual embedding.” As the name suggests, this embedding also holds the information of the context, unlike simple word embeddings, which can’t do that. The self attention layer takes the word embeddings as input, adds contextual information to them, and returns the contextual embeddings.

Now a natural question arises: how does self attention do this? In the next paragraph we will get to know this.

  1. Key(K): What a word offers.

Why are these weight matrices required?

The entire self-attention mechanism relies on Q, K, and V. To understand how strongly one word is related to another, we need these three components.

For example, when processing the word "Although", its Query (Q) is compared with the Keys (K) of all the other words to calculate how much attention it should pay to each word.

But the main question is: Who defines the roles of Q, K, and V inside the model? How does the model know that Q is supposed to make a query? And what does a query actually mean?

The answer is that initially, the model doesn't know any of these things! During training, the weight matrices get updated through backpropagation and gradually learn how to generate useful Queries, Keys, and Values.

Basically, we just define the mathematical formulas, such as:

Q = X × WQ​

Here, WQ​ is a learnable weight matrix. Through training, the model learns the values of these weights, which allows Q, K, and V to develop their respective roles in the self-attention mechanism.

After creating Q, K, and V, our next step is to calculate how much one word is related to the other words. Based on this relevance, we will decide how much attention one word should pay to the other words.

Remember, we discussed the roles of Q and K before. Q (Query) represents what a word is looking for, and K (Key) represents what a word has to offer.

These are the two main components we need to calculate the relevance between words.

Here, relevance means how closely a word's Query matches the Keys of all the other words. The higher the similarity score, the more relevant those words are to each other.

What is Scaling?

Scaling is a technique used to control the magnitude of values in embeddings or other numerical representations to keep training stable and improve the learning process.

In machine learning, scaling helps prevent numerical values from becoming excessively large or small, which can make optimization difficult.

Without proper scaling, we may encounter problems such as unstable gradients, exploding gradients, or vanishing gradients, depending on the model architecture and the operations being performed.

How Do We Perform Scaling?

To perform scaling in the attention mechanism, we divide the dot product of the Query (Q) and Key (K) matrices by the square root of the dimension of the Key vectors (dk​​).

After scaling, we do normalization. But before that, we have to know what normalization is and why we use it.

What is Normalization?

Normalization is a process of scaling values in a vector, matrix, or list so that they lie on a common scale — for example, so that their sum equals 1. For a simple sum-to-one normalization, this is commonly known as normalization using the L1 norm.

Why Do We Use Normalization?

Normalization helps maintain numerical stability and can make machine learning models train more effectively. Depending on the normalization technique used, it can help improve both the stability and speed of training.
